Cycle 1 — IBC & Climate Change

Every row house design starts with the same two forces: the code that governs what you can build, and the climate reality every building now has to answer to.

11.1.311.2.1

Learning Objectives

Understand

Explain the purpose of the IBC and how excess greenhouse gasses connect to the building industry.

Apply

Apply IBC minimum-area, ceiling-height, and egress requirements to your own row house design.

Create

Create a building program, a final schematic design (floor plans and elevations sketched to scale) in AutoCAD, a completed code report, and a study model at 1/8" = 1'-0".

Big Ideas & Essential Questions

11.1 — Residential Building Codes and Safety (IBC)
Big Idea(s)
  • Safety is not only important when we construct our home, but also throughout the lifetime of our homes.
  • Training, awareness, planning, and building codes can prevent hazards and injuries.
Essential Question(s)
  • Why are there laws about how we can build our homes?
  • How do we benefit from equitable access to residential spaces?
11.2 — Residential Sustainability/Energy Efficiency (Climate Change)
Big Idea(s)
  • The construction industry is responsible for 40% of the carbon emissions that influence climate change.
  • Architects must meet the needs of today without compromising the needs of the future.
Essential Question(s)
  • How does using sustainable concepts in the design of a building change the outcome of the project?

Lesson Outline

  • Row House Kickoff
    Introduce the Riverfront Row House Design Challenge — each student as an independent firm. Read the full project brief and the 5 key components. Revit is introduced this cycle too, running in parallel with your AutoCAD-based Row House work — it becomes the production tool for the House project starting Cycle 4.
  • IBC Fundamentals
    Purpose of the IBC. Minimum room areas, ceiling height requirements — habitable rooms vs. kitchens/other rooms.
  • Means of Egress
    Door/window egress requirements, stairway width and rise/run. Toilet/bath/shower clearances.
  • Climate Change & the Building Industry
    History and science of climate change; how the building industry contributes; negative consequences of inaction.
  • Studio Work Day
    Develop your building program and begin schematic floor plans and elevations, sketched to scale in AutoCAD. Revit: interface orientation and new-project setup in a practice file.
  • Studio Work Day (cont.)
    Finalize schematic floor plans and elevations; begin your code report. Revit: practice basic wall/door/window placement in your practice file.
  • Wrap-Up & Quiz Review
    Complete your code report and study model at 1/8" = 1'-0". Cycle Check-In Rubric review. Quiz 1 review session.

This is a rough outline and is subject to change.
