Big Ideas & Essential Questions

11.6 — Social Aspects of Architecture
Big Idea(s)
  • The layout of our towns, cities, and structures have been influenced by many factors throughout history.
  • Re-evaluating traditionally held beliefs regarding home and neighborhood design can create better living conditions for our clients and the community.
Essential Question(s)
  • Why do our towns and neighborhoods look the way they do?
  • Why do people care what their neighbors build?
  • What are the steps you must take when constructing a home in a particular town or city?

Lesson Outline · 7 Days

  • Day 1Mixed-Use Kickoff & Neighborhood Evolution
    Introduce the Mixed-Use Building project — new building type, new site. Vernacular neighborhood evolution: mill towns through New Urbanism.
  • Day 2Mixed-Use Developments
    Mixed-use developments as a housing/neighborhood type in depth — direct tie to this cycle's project.
  • Day 3Permitting, Zoning & FAR
    Permitting and zoning basics; FAR calculation practice using the mixed-use site.
  • Day 4Notable Architects
    Case studies of architects known for mixed-use/urban work.
  • Day 5Studio Work Day
    Complete a zoning/FAR analysis for the mixed-use site. Revit: begin new central model — massing for the mixed-use program.
  • Day 6Studio Work Day (cont.)
    Place program elements (residential above, commercial ground floor) in the massing model.
  • Day 7Wrap-Up & Quiz Review
    Cycle Check-In Rubric review. Quiz 8 review session.

This is a rough outline and is subject to change.
