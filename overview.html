Row House, House, DD Set & Mixed-Use — Project Overview

Four connected design challenges spanning the year: a riverfront row house, a single-family home, its Design Development phase, and a capstone mixed-use building.

Four Projects, Independent Firms

Grade 11 shifts from Grade 10's construction fundamentals into building systems, codes/compliance, sustainability, BIM, and the professional/business side of architecture. Rather than a shared class project, each student operates as their own independent architectural firm across four connected design challenges.

Row House (Cycles 1–3)

A riverfront row house design challenge, drafted in AutoCAD: a building program, schematic design, code report, plans, elevations, building section, site plan, and a final presentation poster with physical models — while Revit is introduced in parallel as a skill-building track. Standards: 11.1 (IBC/ADA/OSHA), 11.2 (Climate/LEED/Alt. Rating), 11.3 (Electrical).

House (Cycles 4–5)

A single-family home on a wooded site, centered on plumbing and HVAC integration plus a client-ready rendering. Standards: 11.4, 11.5.

Design Development Set of House (Cycles 6–7)

Your House design carried forward — not restarted — into Design Development: permitting/zoning, alternative material integration, client communication, and firm structure. Standards: 11.6.1/11.6.2, 11.8.1, 11.9.1/11.9.2.

Mixed-Use Building (Cycles 8–10)

A bigger commission on a new site: alternative construction technique at scale, a simulated bid/RFP process, licensure reflection, and a year-end portfolio. Standards: 11.9.3/11.9.4, 11.8.2, cumulative review.

ROW HOUSE

Special Issue: Residential Building Codes · Sustainable Initiative · Residential Design

Welcome to the Riverfront Row House Design Challenge! As aspiring architects, you've been hired by a forward-thinking developer to design a series of unique row houses along a picturesque riverfront property. This project will span Cycles 1–3 and challenge you to blend creativity, sustainability, and technical knowledge.

HOUSE

Plumbing · HVAC · Rendering

Welcome to the Single-Family Residence Design Studio! As an independent architectural firm, you've been hired by a client to design a custom single-family home on a wooded site. This project spans Cycles 4–5 and pushes you from schematic massing into the real systems that make a house livable.

DESIGN DEVELOPMENT SET OF HOUSE

Permitting & Zoning · Alternative Materials · Client Communication · Firm Structure

Your House design doesn't end at Cycle 5 — it gets more real. As your firm continues representing the same client, you're now moving from schematic decisions into Design Development: the phase where a project actually becomes buildable, defensible, and fundable. This project spans Cycles 6–7.

MIXED-USE BUILDING

Alternative Techniques at Scale · Project Path · Licensure · Final Portfolio

Welcome to your firm's biggest commission yet. A local developer wants a mixed-use building — ground-floor commercial space with residential units above — on an urban infill site. This is a different scale and a different building type than anything you've designed this year, and it's the project your firm will carry through to the end of the year. This project spans Cycles 8–10.

Software Thread

BIM & Advanced Rendering (11.7): not a standalone cycle — threaded across Cycles 1–9's Software Exercise assignments. Cycles 1–3 use it as a parallel skill-building track in practice files (Row House itself is drafted in AutoCAD); from Cycle 4 on, Revit becomes the actual production tool, with each new skill landing exactly when that cycle's project needs it.

Software Progression

The Row House project (Cycles 1–3) is drafted in AutoCAD, continuing directly from Grade 10. Revit is introduced in parallel starting Cycle 1 as a skill-building track — practice files, not production drawings — so that by Cycle 4, when the House project begins, students can transition to producing every deliverable in Revit from the start.
